Reading the error

A probability can be more or less wrong.

Read the outcome: 0 = survived; 1 = died. If the person died, use p(death). If the person survived, use 1 − p(death). This is the probability assigned to what happened. Its negative natural logarithm is the penalty; average the penalties across records.

The example uses one displayed row from shuffle 1. The chart and ranking summarize the full temporal test across repeated shuffles. Log loss evaluates probability predictions; lower is better. ln denotes the natural logarithm. It is not a pure calibration statistic. Displayed values are rounded; calculations retain full precision.
