Study details and numerical evidence

The whiskers show variation across column shuffles, conditional on this fitted model and evaluation set. They are not population confidence intervals. The Brier score averages squared differences between predicted death probabilities and observed outcomes.

Cross-slide numerical audit: notebook §11 ↗

This is permutation importance for predictive performance. It does not establish a causal effect of a variable on death. Ratios below 1 are possible when shuffling reduces the error; 1 is a reference, not a lower bound. The top-ranked features shown here all increase mean error.

Classification notes

Reading the error

A probability can be more or less wrong.

First read the observed outcome: 0 = survived; 1 = died. Then measure how far the predicted probability is from that outcome. Square that distance, and average across records.

The table uses a real record from the teaching subset. Its average is separate from the full test-set score. A probability may get worse without crossing the classification cutoff. Brier measures probability error; it is not a pure calibration statistic. Displayed values are rounded; calculations retain full precision.
